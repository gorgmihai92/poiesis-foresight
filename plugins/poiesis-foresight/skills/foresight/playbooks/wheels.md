# Wheels: the consequences of one change

What follows from one change, and what follows from that: first-order effects, second-order effects, and later
ones, including the ones nobody intended.

## Read first
Notes in `research/notes` of the plugin's repository.
- `go-science-2024-futures-toolkit`: its Futures Wheels section, read in full (p. 75–78). Practitioner guidance.
  The steps below adapt its six steps (Open Government Licence v3.0). Its own warning: "This is a speculative
  tool: all inputs are based in an as yet unknown future" (p. 76).

## Steps (toolkit, p. 77)
1. **One change at the centre:** an event, a trend, an issue, a decision or a concept. Write it in one line, with
   its **From** (a signal, a scenario, a driver, a forecast).
2. **Stand after it.** Describe the situation as if the change has already happened.
3. **First ring: the immediate effects.** Finish them all before going further. Go through the kinds of effect so
   none is skipped: political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental.
4. **Second ring: what each first effect leads to,** attached to its parent. Finish the ring before the next.
5. **Further rings** until the picture stops changing. Give each ring a rough time: within a year, two to three
   years, three to five, later.
6. **Read the whole:** the surprises, the backlashes, the effects that work against the change, and the effects
   that reach the same place by two routes.

Write it as an indented list, one effect per line, each child under its parent.

## Rules
- Every line is a conjecture. Mark the wheel as speculative at its top, in the toolkit's own words or yours.
- An effect is "A leads to B". Say the mechanism in a few words. An effect with no mechanism is removed.
- Look for the effect that undoes the change, and for the one that harms someone the change was meant to help.
- Draw a second wheel separately (another pass, or an agent that has not seen the first) and compare. The toolkit
  compares the wheels of several groups (p. 77); two separate passes are this plugin's stand-in for that, and a
  narrower one.
- An effect that can be checked becomes a forecast in the log. One worth watching becomes a signpost.

## Then
A wheel is not a conclusion. Anything taken from it as one goes through `playbooks/challenge.md`.
