## What it says
- Three patterns of doing things in one area, drawn over time by how prevalent each is (p. 4).
- The first horizon (H1) is the way things are done now, "business as usual"; the conversation starts from the
  recognition that it "is losing its fit with emerging conditions" (p. 4).
- The third horizon (H3) is the emerging pattern that will be the long-term successor; it is "appearing and
  growing on the fringes of the present system" (p. 5).
- The second horizon (H2) is the turbulent domain of transitional activities and innovations. Some (H2+) help the
  third horizon to emerge; many fail; others (H2−) are "absorbed back into the H1 systems and contribute only to
  marginal or incremental change" (p. 5).
- All three exist at once: "each way of doing things is present at all times, albeit to a greater or lesser
  extent" (p. 5).
- Calling an innovation H2 plus or H2 minus "is not absolute but something that can be explored" (p. 8).
- The authors say the approach has limitations, and that other tools are sometimes needed for depth: systems
  mapping, scenarios for the uncertainties of the third horizon, and others (p. 10).
- The abstract notes "limited research on practices for facilitating transformative change" (p. 1).

## What it means for the plugin
- `playbooks/horizons.md` places each pattern as H1, H2+, H2− or H3, and treats the placement as a reading that
  can be argued, not as a measurement.
- A signal's **Horizon** field keeps the four values the files contract already has.
- The framework was written for a group in dialogue. Used by one person with a model, it is a way to sort what
  was found; the dialogue between people with different stakes, which the authors value most, is not reproduced.
- It is a practice described by its authors. Nothing here shows that sorting patterns this way leads to better
  decisions.
