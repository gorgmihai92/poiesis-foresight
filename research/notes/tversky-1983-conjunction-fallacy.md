# Extensional versus intuitive reasoning: the conjunction fallacy in probability judgment (Tversky & Kahneman, 1983)

- **Source:** Psychological Review 90(4): 293–315 · https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.90.4.293
- **File:** none (closed at the publisher; a copy on a university course page was not used, since its licence is
  not stated)
- **Licence:** closed.
- **Evidence:** empirical · judgments of lay people and of experts in several contexts (abstract). Samples are not
  in the abstract.
- **Read:** abstract only, as OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar returned it on 2026-11-22 through poiesis-research's
  `search.py`.

## What it says (from the abstract)
- The conjunction rule: "The probability of a conjunction, P(A&B), cannot exceed the probabilities of its
  constituents".
- Intuitive judgment is not bound by the rule: a conjunction "can be more representative than one of its
  constituents", and so can "appear more probable".
- Violations were observed "in judgments of lay people and of experts", in contexts that include medical
  prognosis and political forecasting.

## What it means for the plugin
- The third question of `playbooks/challenge.md`, "detail adds no probability", has two parts. The arithmetic is
  certain and needs no source: a scenario with more conditions cannot be more likely than the same scenario with
  fewer. That people, experts included, judge the detailed version more likely is this paper's finding, known
  here from its abstract only.
