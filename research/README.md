# Sources behind the rules

The sources behind the rules in `skills/foresight/rules.md` and the playbooks. One note per source in `notes/`.
Each note's **Read** line says how much of the source was read and what was not: full text, part of the text
(with the pages), abstract only, or title only. No rule rests on a source that was not read; a rule whose source
was read as an abstract says so; some rules wait.

Copies of the texts are kept in `papers/` on the machine where they were read and are not in the repository.

**Read in part, with the pages in the note**
- **The frame and the workshop methods:** [The Futures Toolkit 2024](notes/go-science-2024-futures-toolkit.md),
  UK Government Office for Science: scanning, three horizons, drivers, scenarios, wheels, stress-testing. Open
  Government Licence v3.0. Practitioner guidance.
- **Trends and emergence:** [Rotolo, Hicks & Martin 2015](notes/rotolo-2015-emerging-technology.md), five
  attributes of an emerging technology; [Klavans, Boyack & Murdick 2020](notes/klavans-2020-exceptional-growth.md),
  predicting exceptional growth in research.
- **Base rates:** [Flyvbjerg 2006](notes/flyvbjerg-2006-reference-class-forecasting.md), reference class
  forecasting.
- **Forecasts:** [Chang et al. 2016](notes/chang-2016-forecasting-training.md), what an hour of training changed;
  [Halawi et al. 2024](notes/halawi-2024-llm-forecasting.md), a model system against a crowd;
  [Schoenegger et al. 2024](notes/schoenegger-2024-silicon-crowd.md), twelve models against a crowd;
  [Karger et al. 2025](notes/karger-2025-forecastbench.md), models against expert forecasters;
  [Paleka et al. 2025](notes/paleka-2025-evaluating-llm-forecasters.md), why models cannot be tested on the past.
- **Horizons and scenarios:** [Sharpe et al. 2016](notes/sharpe-2016-three-horizons.md), three horizons;
  [Phadnis et al. 2015](notes/phadnis-2015-scenario-planning.md), what scenarios did to experts' judgment.
- **Signposts:** [Walker, Marchau & Kwakkel 2019](notes/walker-2019-dynamic-adaptive-planning.md), signposts,
  triggers and responses; [Haasnoot, Warren & Kwakkel 2019](notes/haasnoot-2019-adaptive-policy-pathways.md),
  timely and reliable signals. CC BY 4.0.

**An observation**
